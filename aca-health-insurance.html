Understanding ACA coverage

What is ACA health insurance?

ACA health insurance refers to individual and family major medical coverage that follows requirements established under the Affordable Care Act. These plans are commonly available through the federal Health Insurance Marketplace or a state-based Marketplace.

ACA plans can be an option for people who do not receive health insurance through an employer, are self-employed, recently lost other coverage, retired before becoming eligible for Medicare, or need to purchase coverage on their own.

Instead of focusing only on the monthly premium, we help you look at how the entire plan works, including deductibles, copays, maximum out-of-pocket costs, provider networks, prescriptions, and the way you expect to use healthcare.

Comparing your options

What should you look at when choosing an ACA plan?

The lowest monthly premium is not automatically the best plan. Your healthcare needs, doctors, medications, household, and financial priorities can all affect which option makes sense.

Monthly Premium

The amount you pay each month to keep your health insurance active.

Deductible

The amount you may need to pay toward certain covered services before the plan begins sharing more of the cost.

Provider Network

Plans can use different doctor, hospital, and healthcare provider networks. Checking your preferred providers can be an important part of comparing coverage.

Prescription Coverage

Drug formularies can vary by plan. Reviewing your medications can help you understand potential prescription costs.

Copays & Coinsurance

These are different ways you may share the cost of healthcare services with your insurance plan.

Maximum Out-of-Pocket

This represents the maximum amount you may pay during the plan year for covered in-network services that count toward the plan's out-of-pocket limit.

Marketplace savings

Can ACA health insurance be more affordable?

Some households may qualify for financial assistance when purchasing coverage through the Health Insurance Marketplace. Eligibility and the amount of available assistance can depend on factors such as household income, household size, location, and other eligibility requirements.

We can help you review Marketplace options and understand how potential financial assistance may affect the plans available to you.

Eligibility for financial assistance is determined by the applicable Health Insurance Marketplace and is not guaranteed.

Enrollment timing

When can you enroll in ACA health insurance?

Most people enroll during the annual Open Enrollment Period. Depending on your circumstances, you may also be able to enroll outside of Open Enrollment through a Special Enrollment Period.

Examples of life events that may affect enrollment eligibility include:

  • Losing qualifying health coverage
  • Getting married
  • Having or adopting a child
  • Moving to a new coverage area in certain circumstances
  • Other qualifying household or coverage changes

Special Enrollment Period eligibility depends on your specific circumstances and Marketplace requirements.

Frequently asked questions

ACA Health Insurance FAQs

Is ACA insurance the same thing as Marketplace insurance?

The terms are often used together. The Health Insurance Marketplace is one place where individuals and families can shop for ACA-compliant health insurance coverage.

Can I buy ACA insurance if I am self-employed?

Self-employed individuals may purchase individual or family health insurance through the Marketplace if they meet applicable eligibility requirements.

Can I enroll if I recently lost employer coverage?

Losing qualifying employer-sponsored health coverage may create a Special Enrollment Period. Eligibility and enrollment deadlines depend on your circumstances.

Can you help me check whether my doctor accepts a plan?

Yes. Provider networks are an important part of comparing health insurance plans. We can help review available network information as part of your coverage comparison.

Can you help me compare prescription coverage?

Yes. Prescription formularies and costs can vary between plans. Reviewing medications before enrolling can help identify important differences between coverage options.
