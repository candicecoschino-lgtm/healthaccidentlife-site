Medicare basics

What is Medicare?

Medicare is federal health insurance primarily for people age 65 and older. Certain younger people may also qualify based on disability or other eligibility requirements.

Medicare has different parts that cover different types of healthcare services, and there are different ways to receive Medicare coverage.

Understanding the basics can make it easier to know what questions to ask when reviewing your individual coverage options.

Coverage options

Understanding Medicare coverage

Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage are the two main ways to receive Medicare coverage. Additional coverage options may also be available depending on how you receive Medicare.

Original Medicare

Original Medicare includes Part A and Part B. Part A generally helps cover inpatient hospital care, while Part B generally helps cover physician services and other outpatient medical care.

Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage, also known as Part C, is an alternative to Original Medicare offered through Medicare-approved private insurance companies.

Medicare Supplement

Medicare Supplement insurance, also called Medigap, works with Original Medicare and can help pay certain out-of-pocket costs that Original Medicare does not pay.

Medicare Part D

Medicare Part D provides prescription drug coverage through private plans. Covered medications and costs can vary by plan.

Provider Access

Provider access depends on how you receive Medicare coverage. Some private Medicare plans use provider networks.

Prescription Coverage

Drug formularies and prescription costs can vary between plans, making your current medications an important part of reviewing coverage.

Comparing coverage

What should you consider when reviewing Medicare coverage?

Your doctors, prescriptions, healthcare usage, travel needs, and current coverage can all be important when discussing Medicare options with a licensed Medicare agent.

  • Which doctors and hospitals do you use?
  • What prescription medications do you take?
  • How frequently do you use healthcare services?
  • Do you travel frequently or live in more than one state?
  • What healthcare costs are important to you?
  • Do you currently have other health coverage?

Enrollment timing

When can Medicare coverage be changed?

Medicare has several enrollment periods. The enrollment period that applies to you depends on your eligibility, current coverage, and individual circumstances.

Common Medicare enrollment periods include:

  • Initial Enrollment Period
  • Medicare Open Enrollment Period
  • Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period
  • Special Enrollment Periods for certain qualifying circumstances

Different enrollment periods allow different types of changes. A licensed Medicare agent can help you understand which enrollment opportunities may apply to your situation.

Frequently asked questions

Medicare FAQs

Is Medicare Advantage the same as Original Medicare?

No. Original Medicare includes Part A and Part B and is administered by the federal government. Medicare Advantage plans are offered by Medicare-approved private insurance companies as another way to receive Medicare Part A and Part B benefits.

What is Medicare Supplement insurance?

Medicare Supplement insurance, also known as Medigap, works with Original Medicare and can help pay certain out-of-pocket healthcare costs that Original Medicare does not pay.

What is Medicare Part D?

Medicare Part D provides prescription drug coverage through private insurance plans that contract with Medicare.

Do Medicare Advantage plans use provider networks?

Provider access and network requirements can vary between Medicare Advantage plans. Checking whether your doctors and hospitals participate in a plan's network can be an important part of comparing coverage.

Can prescription coverage vary between Medicare plans?

Yes. Drug formularies, participating pharmacies, coverage requirements, and prescription costs can vary by plan.

When is Medicare Open Enrollment?

Medicare Open Enrollment runs from October 15 through December 7 each year. During this period, people with Medicare can make certain changes to Medicare Advantage and prescription drug coverage for the following year.
