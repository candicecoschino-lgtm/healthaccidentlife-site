Medicare basics

Understanding the parts of Medicare

Medicare is a federal health insurance program primarily available to people age 65 and older and certain younger people who meet eligibility requirements.

Medicare includes several different parts, and the way those parts work together depends on the type of coverage you choose.

Coverage choices

What are the main Medicare options?

Original Medicare

Original Medicare includes Medicare Part A and Part B. It provides hospital and medical coverage through the federal Medicare program.

Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage plans are offered by private insurance companies that contract with Medicare and provide Medicare Part A and Part B benefits through the plan.

Medicare Supplement

Medicare Supplement insurance, also called Medigap, is designed to help pay certain out-of-pocket costs associated with Original Medicare.

Medicare Part D

Medicare Part D helps provide prescription drug coverage. Coverage and formularies can vary between plans.

Provider Access

Different Medicare coverage options may have different provider access rules and network requirements.

Prescription Coverage

Reviewing medications and plan formularies can be an important part of comparing Medicare options.

Comparing coverage

What should you consider when reviewing Medicare options?

Medicare coverage is not one-size-fits-all. Your healthcare needs, doctors, prescriptions, travel habits, financial priorities, and preferred way of receiving care can all affect which type of coverage may fit your situation.

  • Monthly premiums
  • Deductibles and copays
  • Maximum out-of-pocket costs where applicable
  • Doctor and hospital access
  • Prescription drug coverage
  • Plan networks
  • Coverage while traveling
  • Additional plan rules and requirements

Enrollment timing

When can you enroll or change Medicare coverage?

Medicare has several enrollment periods. Which enrollment period applies depends on your age, eligibility, current coverage, and circumstances.

Common Medicare enrollment periods include:

  • Initial Enrollment Period
  • Annual Enrollment Period
  • Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period
  • Special Enrollment Periods in qualifying circumstances

Enrollment rules and deadlines can vary depending on the type of coverage and your individual circumstances.

Frequently asked questions

Medicare FAQs

Is Medicare Advantage the same as Original Medicare?

No. Original Medicare is administered by the federal government. Medicare Advantage plans are offered by private insurance companies that contract with Medicare.

What is Medicare Supplement insurance?

Medicare Supplement insurance, also called Medigap, works with Original Medicare and is designed to help cover certain costs that Original Medicare does not pay.

Do I need prescription drug coverage?

Prescription drug coverage may be available through a standalone Medicare Part D plan or through certain Medicare Advantage plans that include prescription drug coverage.

Can you help me check my doctors?

Yes. Provider access is an important part of comparing Medicare coverage options.

Can you help review my medications?
